Christian Practices

Christian practices help us remember Christ's finished work, respond to His grace, and share life with His people. Salvation is received by faith alone; practices do not earn forgiveness, secure eternal life, or make God more willing to love us.

This collection gathers five practices, from baptism and Communion to daily devotions; our prayer guides have their own Prayers page. Their names and forms do not make them required Christian observances. In our doctrinal framework v2.1, Article 22, prayer, meditation, and symbols express dependence on God. No formula, gesture, or object guarantees a manifestation. The Holy Spirit is the source of spiritual power.

Baptism

Baptism is a sacrament of initiation into the Christian faith, symbolizing the believer's death to sin and resurrection to new life in Christ. Through water baptism, Christians publicly declare their faith in Jesus and their commitment to follow Him. This practice is rooted in Jesus' own baptism by John the Baptist and His command to "go and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit" (Matthew 28:19).

Baptism witnesses to cleansing and new life already received through faith in Christ. We believe justification and the Spirit's indwelling are God's gifts at regeneration; water and the act of baptism do not earn them.

Key Scriptures: Matthew 28:19, Acts 2:38, Romans 6:3-4
🍞

Communion (The Lord's Supper)

Communion, also known as the Lord's Supper or the Eucharist, is a sacred meal instituted by Jesus on the night before His crucifixion. During this practice, believers partake of bread and wine (or grape juice) to remember Christ's sacrifice—His body broken and His blood shed for the forgiveness of sins. This practice not only commemorates Jesus' death but also anticipates His return and celebrates the unity of the Church as the body of Christ.

We understand Communion as participatory alignment with Christ: receiving the bread and cup with gratitude, remembering His body and blood, and resting in forgiveness already given. The framework associates Communion with prayer for restoration and protection.

Key Scriptures: 1 Corinthians 11:23-26, Luke 22:19-20, John 6:53-56
🙏

Prayer & Fasting

Prayer is the heartbeat of the Christian life—a direct line of communication with God where believers can worship, confess, give thanks, and make requests. Through prayer, we draw near to God, align our hearts with His will, and experience His presence and peace. Jesus Himself modeled a life of prayer and taught His disciples to pray with faith and persistence.

Fasting sets aside food or another ordinary comfort to make room for prayer and attention to Christ. It trains attention and dependence; it does not purchase God's favor, increase our standing in Christ, or compel an answer. A non-food fast can also create time for prayer.

We use fasting to soften our hearts when they have become hardened and to overcome stubborn unbelief rooted in relying only on our natural senses. As we pray and fast, we turn our attention from what we see and feel to what God has said.

We believe the Bible teaches practical wisdom and good common sense. Yet there are times when faith calls us to trust God's Word even when our senses and circumstances seem to contradict it.

2 Corinthians 5:7 (NKJV): “For we walk by faith, not by sight.”

Key Scriptures: Matthew 6:5-13, Philippians 4:6-7, Matthew 6:16-18, Joel 2:12, 2 Corinthians 5:7
🕯️

Anointing with Oil

Anointing with oil is a biblical practice used to set apart individuals for God's service, to signify the Holy Spirit's presence and power, and to pray for healing. In the Old Testament, prophets, priests, and kings were anointed with oil as a sign of God's blessing and calling. In the New Testament, believers are encouraged to anoint the sick with oil and pray for their healing, trusting in God's power and mercy.

The framework describes oil as a physical point of contact in prayer for God's ministry, healing, and protection. We believe it serves as a point of access through which God can reach and minister to the person being anointed. Read with Article 22, this is covenant participation: God remains the source and the Holy Spirit the agent. Oil is neither a charm nor magic.

Key Scriptures: James 5:14-15, Mark 6:13, 1 Samuel 16:13
📖

Daily Devotions

Daily devotions refer to the practice of setting aside time each day for personal Bible reading, prayer, and meditation on God's Word. This spiritual discipline helps believers grow in their knowledge of Scripture, deepen their relationship with God, and find guidance and encouragement for daily life. Regular time in God's presence transforms hearts and minds, shaping us to become more like Christ.

Give particular attention to Jesus' words, actions, and intentions, reading the whole of Scripture in His light. Hold a teaching in mind, consider what it reveals about His heart, and practice one response of love. This renews the mind and aligns daily conduct with life already given in Christ. Missing a devotional time does not break your union with Him.

Key Scriptures: Psalm 1:1-3, Joshua 1:8, 2 Timothy 3:16-17
